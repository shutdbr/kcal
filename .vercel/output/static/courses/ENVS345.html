About

Theatre of the Climate: Performing a Just Future is an interdisciplinary course that bridges environmental sciences and the arts to explore climate change as both a systemic crisis and a human story. As climate realities become increasingly urgent, understanding them requires not only scientific insight but also emotional resonance, cultural memory, and ethical imagination. This course invites students to examine climate change through the lenses of social and ecological justice, considering how inequalities of caste, gender and geography shape vulnerability and responsibility. Blending systems thinking, ecological theory, and participatory art practices in theatre, dance, storytelling, and visual arts, students will explore the role of traditional knowledge systems, in making the climate crisis personal and public. Drawing on readings from science, performance studies, and decolonial thought, alongside engagements with artistic practitioners and community archives, students will develop original art-based interventions. The course culminates in collaborative performance prototypes that embody just and sustainable futures. It is ideal for students pursuing environmental sciences, global arts, or any other discipline with an interest in climate action and creative expression. Theatre, dance, and visual arts can bring to life the narratives of those affected by environmental injustices, offering audiences a visceral understanding of these complex issues. This course is proposed to be capped at 15 students. Learning Outcomes: 1. Students will be able to analyse climate change as a “super wicked problem” using systems thinking and social-ecological frameworks. 2. Students will critically engage with the intersections of climate injustice and social inequality through caste, gender and class lenses. 3. Students will develop and apply performative, visual, and narrative art practices to sense, express, and communicate climate experiences and knowledge. 4. Students will collaboratively design and present creative interventions - such as performances or installations - that address climate justice and imagine alternative futures.
