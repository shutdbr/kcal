About

This course offers readings to understand the dynamics of human mobility across the globe, with a particular focus on borders, ecological change, and development-induced displacement. Drawing on interdisciplinary studies, the course is a sociological and anthropological exploration of mobility, whether by force, necessity, or aspiration, in a world increasingly shaped by border security, ecological crises, geopolitical conflicts, and economic development initiatives. One major theme will be the changing nature of borders and how state policies, international regimes, and border technologies mediate mobility, often creating new forms of exclusion and precarity. The second major focus of the course will address ecological displacement—mobility triggered by climate change, environmental degradation, and natural disasters. Students will examine how environmental stressors intersect with existing vulnerabilities, including race, class, gender, and indigeneity, creating complex patterns of mobility and immobility. The third focus will be on development-induced displacement: the forced relocation of communities due to infrastructure projects, urban expansion, resource extraction, and conservation initiatives. Students will analyse the underlying logics of such displacement, often justified by narratives of progress or national interest, and the uneven power relations that shape who gets displaced, who benefits, and who is left behind. The course, though borrowing readings from all disciplines, is grounded in anthropology as it connects the everyday experience of migration from across the world with questions of power. These are just some of the questions we will ask ourselves throughout the course as students will be encouraged to reflect on the assumptions and concepts that underlie our often strongly held beliefs about migration.
