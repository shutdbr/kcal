About

‘The more data the better’! ‘Data is the new oil’! This course problematizes these maxims by familiarizing students with the politics and paradox of data in healthcare. The course examines how data is produced, enacted, cleaned, refined, represented and utilized to diagnose and prevent disease outbreaks, expand access to healthcare, and determine patterns of funding and resource allocation in global health. In recent years, the term data has been attached to a range of approaches and processes, such as data-driven, data-fied, and data-based systems, to signify the promises that data holds for improving healthcare and advancing development. Central to these approaches are the infrastructures and technologies (digital and otherwise), such as surveys, statistics, satellites, smartphones, apps, algorithms, big data, platforms, and artificial intelligence, that generate, maintain and circulate data from dispersed sites in the pursuit of more precise, predictive, and pre-emptive health outcomes. We ask how these infrastructures reconfigure everyday health work, doctor-patient relationships, and broader understandings of expertise, innovation, accountability, transparency and trust. And, on a more micro level, how digital and data infrastructures encourage new forms of self-care and self-monitoring practices? What modes of health governance do data infrastructures enable and what does that tell us about the role of the state in the delivery of healthcare? What happens when an eclectic mix of new public-private actors (technologists, designers, entrepreneurs, and consultants) enter this ecosystem? And, what does that reveal about underlying power dynamics, inequalities and market interests that animate data-driven healthcare? This course brings together scholarship from science and technology studies, medical and political anthropology, and critical data studies. We will read ethnographic accounts (with a significant focus on India and Africa) as well as policy-oriented documents. This approach situates grounded, empirical insights within broader political and institutional contexts.
