About

In understanding the history of Indian arts, knowing the Deccan, its place and role, is crucial. In this course we will travel the triangular realm of “Dachinabades” as named in the first-century Periplus of Erythraean Sea, exploring its contours through the lens of its artistic traditions,literatures and cultures. Since this exploration will become very ambitious if we were to cover all aspects of Dakhina (Prakrit) arts, we narrow it down to the Early Modern Period (fifteenth-century onwards), a momentous time of which we have an immediate cultural memory, making the study of artistic heritages a traceable historicity. We will study the Deccan’s unique art forms and cultural practices through music, dance, literature, architecture, paintings, food and fabric that reflect the multicultural and cosmopolitan socio-political ambience in which rulers such as Krishnadevaraya, Adil Shah, Raghunatha Nayaka, and Serfoji each played significant roles. Through readings, viewings and other detailed digital archival material we will learn to close- watch performative arts of the early modern era as milestones in the cultural history of India. The aim of the course is to enable students identify the distinct features of each of historical period such as the Vijayanagara, Sultanate, Nayaka and Maratha as well as to have a panoptic view of early modern era’s artistic legacies whose essence manifests in diverse cultural encounters. The course will prod us to think of deliberate omissions and censoring when the nation-state regrouped its artistic heritages. Coursework will include readings, class discussions-seminars and presentation style disseminations requiring high level of class participation, interactive assignments and some writing work. While the course does not demand any art practice background, those who are practitioners of any genre will be encouraged. Course Objectives / Learning Outcomes: To introduce some artistic traditions across genres from the early modern era and help students learn/read the movement of art through its socio-political legacy To work on some aspects of embodiment, wherever possible to add value and inspiration to the course work To interweave with the domain of history bringing a multi-perspective approach to learning the arts, history, and society.
