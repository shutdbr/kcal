About

The process of establishing an identity as a modern democratic sovereign state was not easy for India. Especially because this required the diversity and plurality of identities to be anchored to a common universally accepted idea or imagination of the nation. The history of india is replete with civilisational encounters and invasions. However, India distinctively transformed invasions into accommodation, rupture into continuity and division into diversity as argued by Sunil Khilnani. Thus, the success or failure of the state is deeply contingent on understanding its constraints and compulsions. It is deeply connected to the success of democracy in accommodating and nurturing the diversity of identities. Diverse identities often come in conflict with each other unsettling the democratic fabric of the state which significantly relies on the principles of liberty and equality to defend itself. Thus, the course will engage with the idea and imagination of India though contemporary debates and challenges around the working of Indian politics. Some of the debates that the course identifies are: the paradox between democracy and development, frequently raising the question of equality; caste and the question of social justice that brings under review state performance and affirmative action; religious freedom and the communalisation of politics that demands secularism be understood through democracy, equality and liberty; regionalism and secessionism that challenges territorial state sovereignty while accommodating ethnic diversity; and the universal and uniform language of representation that addresses the women’s question, nonetheless failing to accommodate the inter-sectionalities and diverse lived experiences. The course is divided into five broad units: Imagining India: Democracy and Development; Caste, Social Justice and Reservation; Religion and Communalism; Regionalism and Secessionism; and India and the Women’s Question.
