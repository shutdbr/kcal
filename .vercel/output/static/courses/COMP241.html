About

How does your phone straighten a photograph of a document? How do panorama apps stitch multiple images seamlessly? How do self-driving cars understand depth from stereo cameras? This course bridges the mathematical foundations from Geometry for Computer Vision I to real-world computer vision applications. Building on your solid understanding of projective geometry from Course I, you&#39;ll learn how cameras capture 3D scenes as 2D images and how to reverse this process computationally. Through hands-on programming projects, you&#39;ll implement the algorithms behind everyday vision applications—from document scanning to 3D reconstruction from photos. The course balances mathematical understanding with practical implementation. You&#39;ll derive key algorithms to understand the mathematics, then implement them in Python to see how they work on real data. A substantial optimization primer ensures you understand the mathematical foundations underlying computer vision algorithms—what is a cost function, how gradient descent works, why RANSAC handles outliers. No advanced mathematics required—just the geometry from DATA239/COMP239, linear algebra, basic calculus, and willingness to learn Python. Through weekly programming assignments, you&#39;ll build proficiency so that by course end, you&#39;ll have implemented core algorithms from scratch and built a substantial team project suitable for your portfolio. This course sets the foundation for understanding both classical geometric computer vision and modern learning-based approaches. Whether you&#39;re interested in AI, robotics, AR/VR, or just want to understand the technology you use daily, this course offers a unique blend of rigorous mathematics and hands-on application.
