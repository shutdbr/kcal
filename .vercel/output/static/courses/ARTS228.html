About

Clay working, instrument making, rhythm, and music have been part of human craft and culture since even before the formation of civil societies. From China to Peru, in every ancient culture around the world, clay flutes or ocarinas, clay horns or water whistles, or some other form of musical instrument, have all used the innate resonant quality of fired clay or the space that it contains, to produce musical notes and specific sounds. This practical, hands-on course will start off by introducing students to the basic techniques of working with clay and making hollow forms by hand. Once students have a grasp of the fundamentals, they will explore and create musical instruments, as well as forms that amplify sound, both in the fired as well as the unfired stage of the clay. As a complement to hands-on work with clay, students will also study some of the forms and simple instruments in a range of ancient as well as modern cultures around the world through readings, research, videos, and guest lectures. By the end of the course, students will be encouraged to experiment with creating and attempting to play their own wind, percussion, or string instruments with clay. BASIC MATERIALS AND EQUIPMENT WILL BE PROVIDED IN THE CERAMICS STUDIO, ON CAMPUS. Each student will need to take responsibility to not waste material, and ensure that the tools and work space is respected and maintained well, and also clean up after themselves, according to the instructions given.
