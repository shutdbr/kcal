About

This course provides an introduction to Vedānta, one of the major schools of Indian philosophy. It explores the key concepts of Vedānta as discussed in the three sources (prasthānatrayī) i.e., Upanishads, Bhagavadgītā and Brahmasūtra. It begins with exploring the fundamental concepts of Brahman, ātman, māyā, jagat and jīvanmukti in these three source texts. Vedānta philosophy employs a number of approaches to unfold the identity between the individual and consciousness (the self). We will discuss some of the important approaches, such as, the cause-effect, the three states, the five sheaths and the seer-seen approach in detail. Through these, we gain a deeper understanding of Vedāntic thought. Moving forward, the course discusses the sub-schools of Vedānta. While the non-dual (advaita) Vedanta of Śankarācārya has become synonymous with the whole Vedānta philosophy, we study it in contrast with two other schools, dual (dvaita) Vedanta of Madhvācārya and qualified non-dual (viśiṣṭādvaita) Vedānta of Rāmānujācārya. The idea is to understand that while relying on the same source text, the variety of interpretation and analysis gives rise to a new philosophy within a system. The course gives an insight into the significance of Vedānta within the larger framework of Indian philosophical thought.
