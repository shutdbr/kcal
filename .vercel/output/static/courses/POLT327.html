About

The Constitution of India with 395 Articles that are organized into 25 Parts and 12 Schedules was the handiwork of the Constituent Assembly that comprised 389-members, who came from diverse social, economic and political backgrounds. It was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into effect on 26 January 1950. During its more than seven decades of existence the Constitution of India has so far been amended 105 times. Despite such a large number of amendments the Constitution’s Basic Structure and other essential features, such as Fundamental Rights, remained more or less the same. In a country of vast diversity, divisions around caste, religious and racial identities, and glaring economic inequities, such an endurance of the Constitution is an astounding achievement both of the members, who drafted the Constitution, and the vast Indian populace that took the vision of the former into their stride. But what is overwhelming is the way in which the Constitution came to be formulated in the Constituent Assembly, the arguments and counter-arguments put forward and the justifications made in the making of every Article of the Constitution. This is because prior to 1950 India never had any written Constitution. Of course, during the later phase of its rule in India the British regime had introduced a few administrative related Acts in the written-form. But then these Acts became concerns of the British rulers and the educated elite of the Indian. A vast majority of the Indian population, who were largely illiterates, remained unconcerned with those Acts of the colonial rulers. Besides this, the socio-cultural diversity; divisions around caste, religion, region and language questions, economic inequities and other divergences and differences that we experience in our times were also very much alive and present at the time of the making of the Constitution for the nascent independent India. Therefore, two most pertinent questions to be asked are: despite all those divergences how did the members in the Constituent Assembly come to agree upon a constitutional framework for the future India; and what were the arguments and justifications made in the Constituent Assembly in the making of certain essential features of the Constitution? A critical exploration of these questions would help the students not only to understand the key debates in the Constituent Assembly, essentially it facilitates a critical thinking, a constitutional perspective and a democratic way of finding solutions to numerous problems that plague our current public life.
