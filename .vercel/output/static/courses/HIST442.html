About

This interdisciplinary research-led module explores the cartographical South – from e.g., the port cities of Cape Town, Durban, Chennai, Adelaide and Sydney to the islands of Sri Lanka, Zanzibar and Mauritius. These transoceanic southern spaces are also transhemispheric, so what might be the continuities and connections geographically between southern sites on different latitudinal lines? Sri Lankan historian Sujit Sivasundaram’s marvellous monographs, Islanded and Waves Across the South, suggest more navigational and narrative strategies for conceptualising and rethinking what we count as archives, and what we call the ‘South’. Our goal in this module is indeed visual, archival and cartographical, as we travel across novels, maps, photographs and other materials to register coordinates on coastlines and seascapes. The texts also take us into the heart of the country, e.g., to the interior landscapes of Namaqualand, the Karoo, Flinders Ranges, Pulicat Lake, Yala National Park, and the botanical gardens of Kandy, Kirstenbosch and Kew. Examining the legacies of the ‘networks of empire’ that bring many of these sites and texts together historically and geographically, the module is excellent preparation for further interdisciplinary research on South-South mobilities, and asks questions about colonial legacies, ideas of ownership, belonging, cultural identity, and migration. The current variation of this module, first launched at SOAS in 2014, is inspired by a series of events: the travelling exhibition, Scenes from the South that I curated in collaboration with universities and museums (2020; 2022; 2024); the public programme, ‘Speaking from the South’ at the University of Adelaide in 2024 that was inspired by this work; the Cátedra Coetzee Literatures of the South programme from 2015-2018 at UNSAM in Argentina, led by Nobel Prizewinner J. M. Coetzee; and the crucial forerunner of my more recent research, a 1992 University of London conference, ‘Southern Spaces’, from which the module takes its name.
