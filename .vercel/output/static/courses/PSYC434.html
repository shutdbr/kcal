About

This advanced undergraduate course offers an interdisciplinary introduction to Critical Disability and Inclusion Studies (CDIS), foregrounding disability as a socially constructed and politically mediated category. Moving beyond individual and medicalized understandings, the course approaches disability through frameworks rooted in social justice, political economy, and intersectionality. We begin by examining the historical and cultural construction of disability and ableism, alongside the emergence of critical theories of access, normalcy, and inclusion. The course then explores how developmental norms shape understandings of childhood disability, and how inclusive education can be reimagined through both psychological and pedagogical models. In the clinical module, we turn to psychiatric disability, community mental health, and cultural critiques of diagnosis and medicalization. Intersectional concerns—such as gender, caste, sexuality, age, and race—are woven throughout, enabling students to critically engage with how multiple forms of marginalization structure experiences of disability. We also examine institutional practices, with case studies of inclusion efforts in higher education, such as at Krea University. The course concludes with an analysis of legal frameworks and policy debates in India and globally, grounded in the history of the disability rights movement. Students will engage critically with theory, policy, and practice to design more inclusive and just systems.
