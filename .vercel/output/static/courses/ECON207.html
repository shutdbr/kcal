About

This is a course on the economic history of Modern India, conventionally dated as the period from 1757 to the present. It aims to introduce to the students economic development and policies pursued in India. It attempts to engage students in locating the Indian economy within the world economy and understanding how the relationship has evolved over time. As contesting perspectives exist they will be exposed to them. The course commences with a study of colonial economic policies and the impact they had on India’s economy. In this section students read the work of some major historians. The course then moves on a study of the evolution of India’s economy since 1947, taking the student through the Nehru era, the Indira Gandhi years, the reforms of 1991 and economic performance under the Modi government. In this section the students will read the work of leading Indian economists. The topics covered will include the Colonialisation of India’s economy, the De-industrialisation debate, Land tenure legislation and its enduring consequences, Famines and the Raj, Colonial institutions, the Origins of modern industry, the Nehru era: Planning and its critics, the Green Revolution, initial Liberalisation moves, ‘1991’, the Modi years: initiatives (‘Make in India’, demonetisation) and shocks (Covid 19).
