About

This course offers a critical and interdisciplinary introduction to caste as a structure of power, a mode of social organization, and a lived experience in South Asia. Moving from scriptural origins to colonial codification, from ideological debates to embodied practices, the course unpacks how it continues to organise everyday life, maintain hierarchies, and shape access to dignity, justice and knowledge. Rather than treating caste as a static tradition, the course approaches it as a dynamic system sustained through everyday interaction and institutional design, yet constantly challenged by movements of resistance and assertion. Through a progression of thematic modules, students explore how caste is normalized and contested across time via scriptural codes and colonial enumeration as well as in ideological debates and sensory experiences. The course begins by interrogating the religious and philosophical foundations of caste before examining how colonial governance transformed caste into a system of classification and political identity. It engages with the ideas of Ambedkar and Gandhi to highlight competing visions of reform and annihilation of caste. Moreover, students explore how caste operates through the body, gender, sexuality, memory and humiliation, and how it becomes tangible in practices of food, touch, smell and labour. The final module emphasizes academic responsibility by training students to write about caste critically with attention to standpoint and representation. Drawing from sociology, politics, anthropology, Dalit studies, feminist theory, and cultural studies, the course uses a range of sources—including testimonies, autobiographies, ethnographies, films, and public texts. Emphasizing reflexive learning, it encourages students to connect theoretical debates to lived realities while developing intellectual rigor and sensitivity. The course fosters critical inquiry and social awareness, enabling students to question hierarchies and engage meaningfully with ongoing struggles for equality and dignity in anti-caste movements.
