About

The course comprises a study of the human defense system against pathogens and disease causing agents. It will start with the genesis of the immune system from the lymphoid cells of bone-marrow tissue, and its maturation. It will go on to the molecular basis for determination of self- and non-self, and how an aberration in this process causes the immune system to go rogue and cause auto-immune disease. The course will center around the humoral and cell-mediated immune responses, of their attendant molecular and cellular events, and how the immune system evolves throughout the lifetime of an individual. It will address different aspects of the immune system such as the genetic control of immunoglobulin gene expression, and antibody production, maturation and diversity, and the roles of the thymus, spleen, and lymph nodes. The modulation or suppression of the immune system for tissue and organ graft will be discussed, as will be the reciprocal effects of disease and the immune system, allergies and the inflammatory response, and the use of the immune-system as therapy against cancer and other diseases as drugs or vaccines. The course will also include the mechanisms pathogens have evolved to evade the immune system, and the state of our knowledge of these systems. The reconstitution of the human immune system in mice will be discussed at length to further enable the student’s understanding of the same.
