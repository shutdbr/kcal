About

Natural disasters are among the greatest threats for human lives and quality of life. This course introduces students to almost all the natural hazards that can cause disasters, from well-known frequently occurring ones to extremely rare ones. The course discusses the causes and consequences of natural hazards, efforts undertaken to predict them in advance and the complications of such efforts, in a fundamental level. The causes of natural disasters are understood based on the underlying scientific investigations. To predict them, routine measurement networks and scientific modelling are required. Finally, mitigating their effect depends on the support and efforts provided by the policy makers. Contents: Lithosphere based: Earthquake, Tsunami, Volcano, Landslide, Sinkhole Weather based: water and wind both contribute to natural disasters: Cloud burst, Flood, Hailstorm, Snow avalanche, Draught, Cyclone, Tornado, Dust storm, Pollen transport, Lightning, Forest fire Biological hazard: Pandemics and epidemics Space based: Space weather effects, meteor or asteroid impact, proximal supernova or black hole Role of climate change and other anthropogenic activities in aggravating the hazards
