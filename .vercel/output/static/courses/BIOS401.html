About

The course begins with introducing the plant’s meristem and its histological organization to the students, followed by the anatomy of the leaf and other simple & complex tissue structures and their organization. Water is important to plant life, and other mechanical phenomena happening inside plants, such as diffusion, osmosis, plasmolysis, imbibition, guttation, transpiration, etc., are considered. It introduces the stomata & the mechanism of opening & closing that enable gas exchange between plants and the atmosphere. Identifying the essentiality of nutrients is foregrounded, along with their roles, the mechanism of uptake of nutrients, and the mechanism of food transport within plants. What role does gravity play, and how will plants grow in space? We learn about concepts of photosynthesis and pigment systems/photosystems. Other important phenomena are discussed, such as photophosphorylation, the Calvin cycle and photorespiration in plants. Carbon fixation, indoor and otherwise. How pollution affects them etc. We consider how and why plants go under nitrogen metabolism, how molecular nitrogen fixation happens, and its significance to the world. Plants also need/use hormones for their growth and development. We discuss how growth hormones such as auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid, and ethylene work. We complete the circle by looking at the seed. We introduce students to seed dormancy, its germination, and the concept of photoperiodism and vernalization. We talk about genetically modified plants and seeds (GMOs) and the hazards and ethical issues around them. The genetic basis of the adaptations in plants and how they vary with the types of climates, for example, how do they become resilient to drought? A brief introduction to modern plant biotechnology and gardening is also discussed. Concept integration and awareness about ecosystems, global warming, forests and the plantation of trees
