About

This course offers a critical introduction to the evolution and practice of democratic politics in India, with a focus on the postcolonial state and its institutions. Through a thematic exploration of the Indian state, constitutional vision, institutional frameworks, and contemporary political challenges, students will gain a grounded understanding of how democracy was imagined, built, and contested in India. Beginning with philosophical and historical reflections on the idea of the state, the course situates the Indian experience within broader traditions of political thought. It then traces how India's Constitution emerged as a blueprint for reconciling diversity with unity, exploring foundational principles such as federalism, secularism, and social justice. The course examines how these ideals were institutionalized through the Parliament, Judiciary, and Election Commission—and how they have evolved in response to changing political contexts, including the rise of majoritarianism, debates on federalism under the Modi government, and policies like "One Nation, One Election." Emphasizing both historical depth and contemporary relevance, the course interrogates how India’s democratic project has negotiated its plural society marked by caste, religion, language, and regional identities. Through key political episodes—such as the Emergency, Mandal vs. Kamandal, and hijab and language debates—students will explore tensions between liberty and order, rights and identity, and state power and popular resistance. Engaging with primary texts, constitutional debates, and critical scholarship, the course encourages students to think analytically about the Indian state and its evolving role in shaping democratic citizenship.
