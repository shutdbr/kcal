About

This course covers the fundamental principles of ecology and ecosystem dynamics, relating these with evolutionary processes and introducing concepts of disturbance and resilience. The focus is on the principles of life, how it functions at the population, community and ecosystem levels, relating these with adaptations to unique environmental conditions. This course will be split into four interrelated modules. The first module, population ecology, will deal with the fundamental concepts of habitat and niche, population dynamics (e.g., population growth and regulation) and population structure (e.g., metapopulations). The second module, community ecology, will focus on species interactions (e.g., interspecific competition, predation and mutualism), the nature of communities (e.g., community structure and function). The third module, ecosystem ecology, will take a in depth look at ecosystem structure and function (e.g., energy flow and nutrient cycling), ecosystem dynamics (e.g., ecological succession) and major concepts related to biogeography (e.g., the theory of island biogeography and the major biogeographical zones of India). The final module, conservation biology, will bring together the previous modules and show how the field of conservation biology has harnessed the principles of ecology to manage biodiversity. The course will focus on the principles of conservation (e.g., the design and management of protected areas), ecological restoration and will conclude with some important case studies, frorm India and across the globe, that showcase various conservation/management strategies.
