About

Newtonian mechanics is deterministic and applies to a system with many degrees of freedom. However, propagating a typical initial condition of the many-body system as per the Newtonian dynamical laws is fraught with difficulties, both conceptual and analytical. Luckily, if we are interested in a class of macroscopic properties which correspond to a myriad of microscopic states of the system, one can profitably employ the language of probabilities. Statistical physics of systems thermodynamic equilibrium is such a description. A major success of statistical physics is it provides a dynamical origin to the laws of thermodynamics, though for a restricted class of systems called ergodic systems. The theory, nevertheless, is applicable to all systems, microscopic and macroscopic systems included. This course is an introduction to some of the research questions in statistical physics. Specifically, the course deals with order theorems, critical phenomena and scaling theory.
