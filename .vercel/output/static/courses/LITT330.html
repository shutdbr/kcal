About

This course aims to expand the study of translation to examine adaptation as integral to it. Some theorists (notably Vinay and Darbelnet: 1958) see adaptation as one translation procedure amongst many others; it is seen as a means of attaining ‘situational equivalence’ to deal with issues of cultural transfer that might affect the receiver’s reception or understanding of the text of departure. Whereas, for others (e.g., Gambier: 1992), all translations inevitably involve adaptation, not least because all acts of translation are, simultaneously, acts of rewriting (Lefevere: 1992). In any case, both poles of opinion, in their own ways, underline the importance of adaptation to translation. Adaptations of literary works to cinema [sometimes described as translation from literary language to cinematic language (e.g., by Cahir: 2006)], offer a fascinating opportunity to study this enmeshed nature of translation and adaptation, and to enrich our understanding of both these crucial procedures of cultural transfer. This course will first employ theoretical perspectives to understand processes of translation and adaptation, and to situate them vis-à-vis each-other. As a practical development of this theoretical work, it will then examine the adaptation/translation of works from three literary genres (viz. novel, play and short story) to cinema; by picking up the nuances of cinematic language (montage, mis en scène, camera-pen, auteur-cinema, among others), it will study the translation of the works from words to moving images and sounds.
