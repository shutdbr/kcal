About

This course examines ‘the urban’ as a field of inquiry and theorization from a relational perspective. The course discusses key thinkers and debates in urban studies and analyses from the industrial period, particularly from the mid-19th century, to the contemporary period. The course will enable interdisciplinary thinking regarding urban phenomena, including urban form, social relations and theorization. We will discuss key theoretical insights and shifts in understanding urban relations by situating social contexts historically. The course materials will further disrupt the binary silos of urban and rural studies by interrogating the co-constitution of the urban and the rural, albeit with an emphasis on forms of urban life. A field trip will further provide first hand experience of the rural- urban continuum, and facilitate the theorization of the distinctions therein.
