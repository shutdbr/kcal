About

Gravity was the first of the fundamental forces of nature to be modelled mathematically. Newton’s law of gravitation was our best theory of gravity for over two centuries, and is used even today for launching space probes. Einstein, expanding on his Special Theory of Relativity, proposed the General Theory of Relativity which described gravity not as a conventional force, but as an effect of the curvature of space-time. This could explain the slight shift of planetary orbits from their Newtonian expectations, and the bending of light in the vicinity of a massive object. This theory – our most successful theory of gravity to date – is used to explain phenomena related to black holes, the recently discovered gravitational waves, and has important applications such as GPS technology. Perhaps one of the most interesting uses of General Relativity has been to model the evolution of the Universe as a whole – its expansion since the Big Bang and its possible future fate. This course covers the mathematical prelude to the study of general relativity, the formulation of Einstein’s field equations, and finally some of the applications of this theory.
