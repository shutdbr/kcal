About

What is identity? How do community identities define socio-political practices? Does identity and othering go hand in hand? How do languages shape community identifications? How does popular culture inform identity formation in India? What is majoritarianism and where do we locate the minorities in India? How are diasporic identities constructed and sustained? In the course ‘Communities, Identities and Cultures in India’ we would ask several such questions and seek answers. This course intends to explore social identities and social locations in India from the perspectives of practices, diversities, and hierarchies around those practices. Expanding on the discourse of personal is political, this course aims to analyse how our everyday practices and identifications stem from larger community affiliations, which also imply stereotypes and othering. The course is divided into four parts - in the first part we will contextualise the significance of communities and identities at large, and survey how scholars have located and critiqued community as an analytical concept to investigate what collective identity might mean in India with special reference to ethnic identities, religious identities, colonialism, nationalism, and secularism. The immersive engagement in this section will deep dive into village studies, and engage with different scholarly approaches to the study of culture through festivals, ritual and other forms of community cultural expression. In the second section we will look into social construction of identities, and how community identity becomes a category of experience through the prism of social categories, cultural expression, and language. This section will also capture the pulse of linguistic nationalism as the defining point of identity assertion in India on one hand and English language elitism as an expression of colonial lingering on the other. In the third segment we will examine the role of mediation, media, and identity and community formation in the diaspora. In this section, students will also engage with transnational frameworks and diasporic identities. Having located the politics around community affiliations and collective identities in the conceptual and theoretical contexts, in the fourth and the last segment, students will undertake a small research project.
