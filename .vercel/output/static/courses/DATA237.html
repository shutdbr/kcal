About

This is an introductory course on applied dynamical systems for the data science major. The main aim of the discipline of dynamical systems is to model the phenomenon of dynamics or change that is ubiquitous in the real world. The earliest examples of dynamical systems addressed by humankind were the motion of planets and other celestial bodies, changes in seasons, and observations of the life cycles of plants and animals. Though it is impossible to track the change in each and every aspect of a given system with many dynamic parts, overall patterns persist that capture gross features of the system. Examples include periodic recurrences of phenomena, irreversibility of certain processes, and so on. The theoretical model of a dynamical system aims to capture such overall global features of the system in mathematically precise terms. An important idea that pervades the study of dynamical systems is chaos: the sensitivity of a dynamical system to initial conditions. The phenomenon of chaos is indeed the norm rather than the exception in the real world, and it appears in a variety of forms with different mechanisms as well. In this course, we will give an introduction to chaos and its importance in understanding the time dependence of real-world phenomena. A good understanding of the basic concepts in Calculus and Linear algebra is encouraged, but the course is designed to be self-contained and will include a review of the important and relevant concepts. The emphasis will be on visualizing the important ideas rather than on rigorous proofs. The course also aims to give a flavour of the interdisciplinary nature of the discipline via many case studies in biology, electric circuits, mechanics, finance, and so on.
